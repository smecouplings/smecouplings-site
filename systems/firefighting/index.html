Pick the pattern first

Storz is the pattern most of western Europe uses — Denmark, Germany, Austria, Switzerland, Sweden and the Netherlands — a quarter-turn internal-lug coupling invented by Guido Storz in 1882. Britain uses the instantaneous coupling to BS 336, France the DSP to NF S61-704 and -705, Spain the three-lug Barcelona to UNE 23400, and Japan its own quarter-turn pattern. None of them interchange.

Aluminium for the coupling, brass for the valve

Quick couplings have to be strong and light at the same time, which is why they are aluminium alloy. Brass and bronze go where the part is a valve, a landing valve or a fitting that lives in salt water — the marine trade pays for gunmetal because it lasts.

Forged, not cast

Cast bodies are fine for irrigation. Fire service is harder on the part: drop it, drag it, pressurise it, repeat. Forged bodies are what we recommend where the coupling is going into service rather than into a garden.

Sizes worth knowing

2″ and 2½″ are the volume sizes. Bigger bores up to 6″ (DN 150) take heavier machines and more engineering to hold tolerance — they are made, but not on the same line.
