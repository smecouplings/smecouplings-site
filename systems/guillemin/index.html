Symmetric couplings

The Guillemin coupling — demi-raccord symétrique, to give it its French name — is a symmetrical claw fitting. Two identical halves meet face to face; a quarter turn of the locking ring draws the chamfered lugs in and closes the joint. It is the standard coupling on tank trucks and bulk transfer lines across France and much of Europe, and it works from PN 6 to PN 16.

The claws are the part that fails

A Guillemin joint only holds while two claws grip the latch lugs on the opposite half. Drop a coupled pair on concrete, or pull the hoses sideways, and either the claw or the lug can crack. Once one goes, the joint opens and the line dumps its contents.

So the claws are ribbed — through the middle and along the sides — and tested to French NF procedure. In the flaring test a 45° hardened steel cone is pressed onto the head until the claws spread and break, which gives the limit. In the drop-cup test a 135° steel cap is dropped onto the head, a few hundred times, until the claw circle closes up or the base starts to split. Ribbing is what buys the extra margin in both.

Latched and unlatched are not the same part

A standard Guillemin closes on two flat rubber gaskets — rubber against rubber. NBR covers water and oils, EPDM chemical and hot service, FPM solvents and cold.

A no-latch coupling has no gasket groove at all: it seals metal against the gasket of the latched half it mates with. It will not seal on its own, and it cannot be paired with another no-latch coupling.

Our internally threaded versions are made without a sealing ring, so wrap the mating external thread with PTFE tape before screwing on. Where a customer wants a seal in the coupling itself, we can fit one to the BSP parallel thread.

Caps and plugs are not pressure vessels

A Guillemin dust cap or plug is a closure, not a blanking plate. The standard expects it to be secured — a padlock through chains and hooks — before a line is left under pressure, and the securing hardware should carry at least ten times the weight of the plug.

Every coupling in the range has chain holes, except the caps and plugs where they sit on the top and middle of the body instead of in the claw ribs. Hole positions can be moved if your handling equipment needs it.

How the hose end is assembled

Crimping clamps are the standard assembly on Guillemin hose couplings. Shanks come with multiple serrations and one-clamp space, or as DIN smooth and serrated tails with a safety collar for DIN safety clamps to EN 14420-3.

Shanks with a collar are reduced bore; the plain ones are full bore. A crimping collar is an option where a 45° or 90° ferrule has to be used, and the bore and collar arrangement can be changed for a specific line.
