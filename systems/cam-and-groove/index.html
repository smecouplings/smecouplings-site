Cam & Groove couplings

Cam and groove — “camlock” for short — is the quick coupling most transfer lines settle on. A cam handle pulls an adapter against a gasket, so a joint opens or closes by hand in a second. Twenty-two items in the range; six of them cover most jobs.

Two standards sit behind it: A-A-59326 (the standard that replaced MIL-C-27487F) and DIN EN 14420-7, formerly DIN 2828. A coupling from one will not seat in the other.

Two standards behind one shape

Camlock halves are built to one of two systems. A-A-59326 is the American one: it replaced MIL-C-27487F, and its types A to F, plus dust caps and plugs, are what most buyers picture when they say camlock. DIN EN 14420-7, formerly DIN 2828, is the German one. The cam groove and the sealing face sit differently, so halves from the two systems do not mate.

Neither standard covers 1/2″, 5″ or 8″. Those sizes are made to each manufacturer’s own drawing and rarely interchange — worth a check before ordering a replacement for a line that was assembled somewhere else.

What makes the joint tight

Three things. The pin holes in the female shoulders have to be in the right place. The shoulders have to be symmetrical, so both handles ride the adapter groove and close level. And the gasket has to be compressed by the right amount for its hardness. Miss either of the first two and the handles will not close; miss the third and they close but the joint weeps.

A camlock should latch under ordinary hand pressure, without a tool — that is how the standard is written. We drill the pin holes in pairs on a two-head machine so both sides match, and check every gasket batch on a durometer.

The seal decides the pressure

Camlock is rated for liquids and dry bulk. Not liquefied gas, and not steam. The body is far stronger than the joint, so working pressure follows the gasket material, and the figures assume two couplings mated at 21 °C (70 °F). Polypropylene ratings come from our own testing — expect them to differ between makers. Each product page gives the value for its size and material.

What we change on request

The patterns keep machining allowance, so the standard shapes can be adapted without new tooling:

  • Ends changed to NPT thread, butt or socket weld, flange, or a screw drill for concrete transfer.
  • Hose shanks in other profiles, to suit a different clamping system.
  • Sealing packs beyond the standard square-section NBR and EPDM — an open-envelope PTFE jacket over a core gasket, for a medium or temperature the standard seal cannot take.
  • Bodies in Hastelloy C-276 or nylon, and hot-stamped aluminium handles where weight matters.
  • Self-latching arms where a joint must not open by accident.

Hose shanks can also be supplied with extra serrations or barbs and an optional collar, so one shank works with crimped ferrules and with worm-drive or superior clamps.
